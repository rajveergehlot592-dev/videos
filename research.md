## **4. Audience psychology: how to hold attention**

YouTube explicitly says Shorts recommendations consider whether viewers choose to watch or ignore a Short, how long they stay, average view duration, average percentage viewed and viewer-satisfaction signals. It also accounts for topic interest, competition and seasonality.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://support.google.com\&sz=32)

YouTube Help+1



That gives us a practical editing framework.

## **A 25-second Short, designed beat by beat**

0–1 sec

**The visual hook**

Start on the most interesting frame. Show the conflict, unusual detail or question immediately. No logo animation or long introduction.

1–4 sec

**The promise**

Use one short line of text or narration to establish what the viewer will learn or what you are attempting.

4–12 sec

**The setup**

Show only the actions required to understand the event. Cut pauses, unnecessary driving and repeated explanations.

12–20 sec

**Escalation and evidence**

Build toward the result. Use a useful close-up, replay or sound cue only when it clarifies the action.

20–24 sec

**The payoff**

Deliver the result, punchline or discovery. Do not hide the answer behind a long outro.

24–25 sec

**The next reason to watch**

End cleanly or connect naturally back to the opening. An optional short series label can encourage return viewers.

These exact time divisions are our initial creative hypothesis, not a scientifically established formula for viral videos. Some clips will work better at 12 seconds, while an unusual experiment may deserve 40 seconds.

### **Hooks worth testing**

Use the hook that matches the footage.

| Hook style        | Example                                               |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Curiosity         | “I noticed something strange about this interaction.” |
| Experiment        | “I tested whether this actually works.”               |
| Challenge         | “One rule: I cannot use the usual escape route.”      |
| Unexpected result | “The second attempt went differently.”                |
| Visual discovery  | “Pause here and look at the reflection.”              |
| Comparison        | “Same action, different result. Watch carefully.”     |

Avoid fake scarcity, misleading claims and hooks that promise something the video never shows. Curiosity is useful only when the payoff rewards it.

## **5. Editing style: fonts, captions, cuts and effects**

I would not build a generic over-edited gaming channel with flashing text every second. Instead, create a visual identity viewers can recognise immediately.

**Typography**

Use one bold display font for the hook and one highly readable font for spoken captions. Fonts such as Anton, Bebas Neue and Inter are candidates; check the licence files before packaging them in a commercial workflow.

**Framing and movement**

Export at 1080 × 1920 as a starting preset. Use dynamic cropping when the important action can be followed without losing context; otherwise use landscape footage over a blurred vertical background.

**Effects and sound**

Use straight cuts to remove dead time, restrained zooms to direct attention, occasional slow motion for an important moment and a subtle impact effect where appropriate.

Use properly licensed sound effects. Avoid adding transitions simply because an effect is available; every edit should improve clarity, suspense or humour.

**The rule:** the first frame and the event itself must remain understandable even when the viewer watches on a small phone. Effects should support the footage rather than conceal weak footage.

Also remove or mute in-game radio tracks where possible and use appropriately licensed music. Game footage, music rights and commercial-use permissions are separate concerns.

## **6. Free organic promotion: how to reach a worldwide audience**

Paid advertisements are not necessary to begin testing distribution. However, there is no legitimate free-ad switch that guarantees reach.

I would use YouTube Shorts as the main platform for growth and measurement, and publish adapted, watermark-free versions to Instagram Reels. Other distribution channels should be added only where the account and platform rules permit them.

**YouTube Shorts — main audience engine**

Publish your original edits. Use descriptive titles for searchable experiments, organise related videos into series and use the Shorts feed to test broad audience appeal.

**Instagram Reels — secondary distribution**

Upload a clean master, test a platform-appropriate caption and cover, and use replies to turn genuine viewer questions into new videos.

**Communities and collaborations**

Participate in GTA VI communities where self-promotion is allowed. Share a useful experiment or discovery and engage in the actual discussion rather than dropping unsolicited links. Collaborate with other creators through original material or explicit permission.

**Search and emerging topics**

Monitor questions viewers are asking, recurring search terms and new mechanics people are trying to understand. Make your own demonstration promptly. Treat third-party trend trackers as leads to verify, not unquestionable evidence.

Never use artificial views, automated scrolling, engagement exchanges or mass comments as a growth strategy. YouTube explicitly excludes fake Shorts views from monetisation eligibility and prohibits manipulative spam practices.&#x20;

## **9. Everyday analytics: the engine behind the plan**

This is where Antigravity can give you a real advantage. Instead of manually remembering which videos performed well, we can build an analytics log and a daily report that compares every clip with previous clips in the same format.

For every Short, record:

| Metric                                      | What it tells us                                                |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Shown in feed                               | Whether YouTube actually presented the Short in the Shorts feed |
| Stayed to watch / viewed versus swiped away | Whether the opening made people stop                            |
| Engaged views and average view duration     | Whether people continued watching                               |
| Average percentage viewed                   | Whether the video held attention relative to its length         |
| Likes, comments and shares                  | Whether viewers responded to the content                        |
| Subscribers gained                          | Whether the Short helped build a channel, not just a view count |
| Country and subtitle language               | Which audiences and languages are responding                    |
| Results at 24 hours, 72 hours and 7 days    | Whether a video continues to gain traction after publishing     |

YouTube documents these metrics in its [Shorts analytics guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?utm_source=chatgpt.com) and [reach analytics guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314355?utm_source=chatgpt.com).&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://support.google.com\&sz=32)

YouTube Help+1



The daily report should interpret the results rather than merely display numbers:

**Many feed impressions, few people stay**

Test a clearer first frame, a shorter opening sentence or an immediately visible action. The problem may be the hook rather than the overall topic.

**People stay, but leave halfway**

Inspect the timeline to find dead time, confusing narration or a payoff that arrives too late. Cut only what does not help the story.

**Good views, few subscribers**

Give the channel a more recognisable identity. Make each clip part of a clear series so viewers understand why another video from the same channel is worth watching.

**One format outperforms the others repeatedly**

Make new videos using the underlying idea, but investigate a different question or outcome each time. Don't upload the same clip repeatedly with trivial changes.

Do not judge a channel from one viral hit—or delete a Short simply because it underperforms during the first hour. Compare similar formats across multiple uploads and allow the 72-hour and seven-day reports to mature.

## **10. A lean 30-day launch plan**

I would divide the first month into four phases, subject to resolving the commercial-rights question.

1. **Days 1–3: Rights and setup**

   Request written commercial-use clarification. Set up YouTube Studio and Instagram, select a channel identity, configure capture and export tools, and define a clean original-footage workflow. Build the analytics sheet before publishing.
2. **Days 4–10: Discover the best format**

   Produce two carefully reviewed original Shorts daily. Test different hooks, story structures and editing styles. Keep the underlying topic reasonably consistent while varying one creative element at a time.
3. **Days 11–20: Run controlled improvements**

   Compare the first batch at 72 hours and seven days. Focus on the formats with better watch behaviour, subscriber conversion and genuine audience interaction. Test posting windows without assuming a universal best time.
4. **Days 21–30: Build a repeatable series**

   Create follow-up experiments based on real viewer questions. Standardise the export templates, captions, naming, review and reporting. Use the data to decide what to scale and what to stop.

### **The zero-cost tool stack**
